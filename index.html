About Me

It’s me, Mouad Kacimi. I was born in 2005 in a small village called Rissani, though I prefer to call it Sijilmassa, its historical name. Unlike Rissani, which lacks deep historical meaning, Sijilmassa holds significant historical value. I was born on a Friday, the moment when everyone was gathered at the mosque for prayer. That was the start of my life’s journey. I strongly believe that my existence is not random—I feel that I am meant to make a difference, both personally and socially. Looking back, I can confidently say that I have taken one of the best academic paths among my peers, especially by taking on the challenge of preparatory classes, an experience worth discussing. These intense years have shaped me significantly. I still remember my first day at IBN TAHER preparatory classes in Errachidia. I was accompanied by my cousin, Munim, who helped me carry my luggage to the dormitory. That day was terrifying for me because of all the fearful stories we had heard about prépas in general, as well as my brother’s difficult experience there, which even forced him to repeat a year at IBN TAHER. To be honest, prépas were extremely tough for me. Even though I had a strong background in mathematics and sciences, the level of competition was intense—everyone was pushing their limits to be at the top of the class. By the end of the first year, exhaustion took over. I remember a time when the entire class was so drained that we collectively asked our physics and chemistry teacher to cancel the final exam. At first, he refused. But to our surprise, on the day of the exam, he announced its cancellation and instead continued with a lecture on crystallography. The real reason behind this exam cancellation was actually linked to someone special—a friend. You know that kind of friendship that forms when you enter a new school or university, someone who becomes your companion through the journey? That person for me was Nabil, a brilliant and reasonable guy from Errich. Nabil and I entered prépas together from the same first waiting list. From the very beginning, he was determined to get into a top French engineering school, and for that, he needed to transfer to Meknès for the second year. Meknès had a reputation for producing top students who secured spots in France. To succeed in this plan, he needed high grades in math and physics during the first year. That’s why he persuaded our physics teacher to cancel the final exam—because he wasn’t ready for it. In the end, he successfully transferred to Meknès. As for me, I was also interested in changing centers, but not with the same determination as Nabil. However, I eventually managed to transfer to Fès—a phase of my journey filled with many details that I’ll leave out for now to keep this story concise. My Journey Continues: From Fès to INPT Now, I am in Fès, a Moroccan city known for its lack of security. Honestly, I was afraid of how I would adapt and uncertain about my decision. However, I eventually met someone who had also transferred to Fès from the Guelmim center—his name was Anas. He was an amazing guy—studious, determined, and with a clear goal of continuing his studies in France. And he made it! Today, he is studying at a school called MatMéca. Thanks to him, I finally found a good environment where I could adapt. Oh, and I forgot to mention—Anas was my roommate. Step by step, I started making new and great friends, beginning with Amin, a really kind person, all the way to Imad, who I think was the last person I got to know. What truly strengthened my friendship with these people was football. We regularly played matches at the dormitory, which brought us all together. I met some amazing people along the way, including Anas, Sofian, Zouicha, Mouad Echaal, Lmokh, Imad, Taha, Omar, Amin, Salah, Mouad, Zakaria, and many others. The Exam Disaster: A Dream Almost Shattered Moving forward in my story, nearly everyone in my group aimed to take the French engineering school entrance exams, and of course, I was one of them. We all went to Meknès to take the CCP (Concours Communs Polytechniques). All my friends were there. The first exams went normally until the French exam—that’s when my worst nightmare happened. There’s a saying: "Life is tough", and that day, I truly experienced it. While I was in the exam room, with nearly 150 students around me, my phone rang. The result? I got a penalty report (PV) in front of everyone. At that moment, I thought my dream of studying in France was over, especially after the crazy efforts I had put in. From that day until the results were announced, I was in a deep depression, feeling completely defeated. The Final Results & My Path to INPT Finally, the results of all the entrance exams I took were published. The outcome? ✅ Admitted to CCP ❌ Not admitted to Mines-Ponts ✅ Admitted to CNC So now, I am at INPT (Institut National des Postes et Télécommunications), an IT-focused engineering school in Morocco, where I am currently pursuing my studies in Cloud & IoT. This journey has been full of challenges, but each step has shaped who I am today.
